# Level Designer — Best Practices

## Focus
Builds individual playable spaces that teach mechanics, control pacing, and guide players — translating a game's core mechanics and story beats into concrete, playable geometry and encounters.

## Best practices
- Build every level around a central mechanic (movement, stealth, combat, puzzle) and use the space to reinforce that mechanic specifically, rather than being a generic mechanic-agnostic container.
- Teach mechanics environmentally, not through text: show a locked door and a nearby switch and let the player draw the connection, then escalate — repeat the mechanic in new contexts with added complexity over time.
- Design a clear "golden path" — the most direct, always-available route from start to finish — even in levels that support exploration or multiple approaches.
- Pace deliberately: alternate peaks (intense action/challenge) with troughs (exploration, rest, story beats) — the troughs matter as much as the peaks for a level not feeling exhausting.
- Use environmental variety to signal progress and refresh attention — a visual/tonal shift (forest to meadow, etc.) can double as a soft checkpoint and a chance to introduce a new twist on the mechanic.
- Playtest with fresh eyes constantly — a designer who built the level can't judge if the "obvious" path or cue actually reads to a first-time player.
- Use lighting, framing, and composition (not just geometry) as active guidance tools — players should want to look where you need them to go.

## Common pitfalls
- Relying on text prompts or UI markers to teach something the level layout should teach through placement and framing.
- Flat pacing — constant intensity with no troughs, or constant calm with no peaks — both read as monotonous over a full level.
- No clear golden path in an open level, leaving players lost rather than pleasantly free to explore.
- Reusing the same encounter/puzzle shape repeatedly without escalating complexity, so the level goes stale mid-way through.
- Skipping fresh-eyes playtesting and shipping based only on the designer's own familiarity with the space.

## Tools & techniques
- Greybox/whitebox blockouts to validate flow, pacing, and readability before art pass investment.
- Golden-path mapping alongside a full space-usage map (critical path vs. optional/secret areas).
- Peak/trough pacing charts plotted across a level's runtime to visualize intensity distribution.
- Environmental teaching sequences: introduce mechanic in a safe controlled space, then repeat with escalating stakes/complexity.
