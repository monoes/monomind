# Finance Tracker — Best Practices

## Focus
Keeps ongoing visibility into money in and out — expenses, budgets, and core financial KPIs — so decisions are made on current, accurate data rather than stale or informal impressions.

## Best practices
- Record transactions and expenses in real time (or as close to it as possible) rather than batching reconciliation — delayed entry is the single biggest source of inaccurate financial pictures.
- Separate expense categories clearly (fixed vs. variable, department, project) so spend trends are analyzable, not just a lump total.
- Track a small set of core KPIs consistently rather than a sprawling dashboard: profitability (margin, burn), liquidity (cash runway, current ratio), and efficiency (cost per unit of output) at minimum.
- Reconcile budget-to-actual on a fixed cadence (weekly/monthly) and flag variances early rather than discovering drift at quarter-end.
- Keep an audit trail on every entry — source document, approver, category — so figures can be traced and trusted, not just totals.
- Flag anomalies (unexpected spend spikes, missing categorization, duplicate entries) proactively rather than waiting for a periodic review to surface them.
- Present financial status in terms decision-makers act on — runway remaining, budget variance, trend direction — not raw ledger dumps.
- Keep personal/unrelated spend and business finances strictly separated to avoid muddying every downstream metric.

## Common pitfalls
- Batching data entry infrequently, which produces a financial picture that's always weeks stale by the time anyone looks at it.
- Tracking too many KPIs at once, diluting attention from the few that actually predict trouble (cash runway, margin trend).
- Reporting totals without variance context — a number alone doesn't tell anyone whether it's good, bad, or expected.
- Letting miscategorized or duplicate entries accumulate uncorrected, silently corrupting trend analysis over time.

## Tools & techniques
- Budget-vs-actual variance reports on a fixed cadence, with variance thresholds that trigger explicit flags.
- Core KPI set: gross/net margin, cash runway, burn rate, cost per unit — tracked as a trend line, not a single snapshot.
- Categorized expense tracking (fixed/variable, by department/project) to support cohort-style analysis of where spend is concentrated.
- Anomaly checks (spend spikes, duplicate/missing entries) run automatically before data is reported as final.
