# Email Marketing — Best Practices

## Focus
Designs and writes email sequences — welcome series, nurture, onboarding, re-engagement, and B2B cold outreach — that read as human and drive action without feeling like a sales machine.

## Best practices
- One email, one job: a single primary CTA per send, no competing asks.
- Earn the right to sell — lead every sequence with value before the ask.
- Write "you/your" more than "I/we"; open with the reader's world, not your announcement.
- Keep subject lines clear over clever, 40-60 characters, no "I hope this finds you well."
- Match sequence length to intent: welcome (5-7 emails/12-14 days), nurture (5-10), onboarding (5-10 emails to first value), re-engagement (3-5 with a sunset).
- For cold outreach, anchor on a real trigger or signal (hiring, funding, product change) before making the ask.
- A/B test subject lines, send times, and CTA variants — don't guess at what resonates.
- Read every draft aloud; if it sounds like copy, rewrite it.

## Common pitfalls
- Cramming multiple CTAs into one email, diluting the intended action.
- Writing generic, un-personalized cold email that reads as mass-blasted.
- Letting nurture sequences drift into pure promotion with no education or trust-building.
- Ignoring deliverability hygiene — unsubscribe rate above 0.5% or spam-trigger language — until sender reputation is already damaged.
- Skipping the sunset step in re-engagement sequences, continuing to email unresponsive contacts indefinitely.

## Tools & techniques
- Sequence frameworks: Observation→Problem→Proof→Ask and Trigger→Relevance→Value→Ask for cold email.
- Segmentation by behavioral trigger, not just static list membership.
- Success benchmarks: 35%+ open rate (warm), 15-25% (cold), 5-10% reply rate (cold), <0.5% unsubscribe per send.
- Preview text as a second subject line — always write it deliberately, never leave it to auto-pull body text.
