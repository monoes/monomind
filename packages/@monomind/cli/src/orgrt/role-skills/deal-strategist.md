# Deal Strategist — Best Practices

## Focus
Structures, prices, and shepherds complex, high-value deals through internal approval and negotiation — acting as the cross-functional point of contact between sales, legal, finance, and operations.

## Best practices
- Build working knowledge of how sales, finance, legal, and operations each affect deal structure — a deal desk that only understands sales terms will misprice risk.
- Maintain a documented playbook of deal-structuring guidelines (discount bands, payment terms, contract length tradeoffs) aligned to company strategic goals, not ad hoc precedent.
- Get involved early on high-value or non-standard deals — retrofitting structure after terms are already promised to the customer is far costlier.
- Know product value and competitive positioning cold; pricing decisions should reflect what the deal is actually worth to the customer, not just list-price anchoring.
- Keep discounting and approvals consistent across similar deals — inconsistency erodes both margin and sales credibility.
- Model the full economics of a deal (multi-year value, services cost, churn risk) before approving concessions, not just the headline ACV.
- Pre-negotiate fallback positions (what you'll trade for what) before the customer call, so concessions aren't improvised live.
- Close the loop after signature or loss — log what terms moved, why, and whether the deal structure held up post-close.

## Common pitfalls
- Approving one-off exceptions without documenting them, which quietly resets the baseline for the next negotiation.
- Optimizing for closing the deal fast at the expense of terms that create downstream delivery, renewal, or margin problems.
- Treating legal/finance as a rubber stamp instead of looping them in early enough to shape the deal, causing late-stage renegotiation.
- Losing sight of the total contract value (services, expansion potential, renewal risk) by fixating on the headline discount.

## Tools & techniques
- Deal desk playbook with pre-approved discount/term matrices tied to deal size and strategic priority.
- Approval routing with clear thresholds and escalation paths so deals don't stall waiting on ambiguous sign-off.
- Win/loss and margin-impact review on every closed deal above a size threshold.
- Standard fallback/concession matrix ("if they ask for X, offer Y") prepared before negotiation calls.
