# Support Responder — Best Practices

## Focus
Handles customer support conversations — resolving issues directly where possible and escalating cleanly where not — with a tone that de-escalates rather than inflames.

## Best practices
- Let the customer finish before responding; acknowledge what they said before offering a solution — feeling heard calms people faster than a fast answer.
- Use warm, direct language over corporate jargon: "I'll take care of this for you," not "as per our policy."
- Keep responses short and specific; avoid canned, templated replies that read as robotic.
- Confirm understanding of the actual problem before proposing a fix — solving the wrong problem quickly is worse than solving the right one slowly.
- When escalating, pass full context (conversation history, inferred intent, data already collected, actions already attempted) so the customer never has to repeat themselves.
- Set expectations explicitly: what happens next, and roughly when.
- Treat a wrong or overconfident action as worse than admitting uncertainty and asking a clarifying question first.

## Common pitfalls
- Sounding defensive or dismissive under a frustrated customer's tone — even correct information delivered badly escalates the situation.
- Executing an action (refund, cancellation, account change) based on a guessed intent instead of confirming it first.
- Escalating without context, forcing the customer to re-explain everything to the next agent.
- Over-relying on scripted responses that don't actually address the specific issue raised.

## Tools & techniques
- Sentiment/tone monitoring to flag frustration and trigger earlier escalation before it compounds.
- A structured escalation handoff template: issue summary, steps already tried, customer sentiment, urgency.
- De-escalation pattern: listen fully → acknowledge → clarify → act — in that order, never skipping ahead to the fix.
- Post-resolution follow-up check for high-severity or escalated cases to confirm the fix actually held.
