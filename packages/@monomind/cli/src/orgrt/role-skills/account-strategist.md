# Account Strategist — Best Practices

## Focus
Owns the long-term growth and retention plan for an existing key account — turning a single sale into an expanding, durable relationship built on demonstrated value.

## Best practices
- Prioritize accounts by lifetime value potential and strategic fit, not just current spend — some small accounts have outsized growth or reference potential.
- Build a written account plan per key account: stakeholder map, goals, expansion opportunities, and renewal risk — reviewed on a set cadence, not only when a renewal is imminent.
- Invest real time understanding the client's business and industry, not just their usage of your product, so recommendations reflect their actual priorities.
- Map and cultivate multiple relationships inside the account (champion, economic buyer, end users) — single-threaded accounts are the highest churn risk.
- Proactively surface ROI and success stories on a regular cadence rather than waiting for the customer to ask "what am I getting for this."
- Coordinate cross-functionally (support, product, success) so the account has one coherent voice instead of conflicting signals.
- Watch engagement and usage signals continuously to catch risk early — don't rely on the customer to raise a flag.
- Treat renewal and expansion as outcomes of ongoing value delivery, not a separate negotiation event that starts 60 days out.

## Common pitfalls
- Single-threading — relying on one champion who can leave, get reassigned, or go quiet, leaving no account visibility.
- Only engaging the account near renewal time, which reads as transactional rather than invested.
- Treating every account with the same playbook regardless of size or strategic value, spreading attention too thin on low-value accounts.
- Failing to quantify and communicate value delivered, so the customer's mental model of ROI stays vague and easy to challenge at renewal.

## Tools & techniques
- Stakeholder/relationship maps updated quarterly, with explicit champion redundancy plans.
- Account health scoring combining usage, engagement, support sentiment, and executive relationship strength.
- Quarterly business reviews (QBRs) built around the customer's goals, not a generic feature update.
- Expansion opportunity tracking tied to observed usage gaps or stated strategic initiatives.
